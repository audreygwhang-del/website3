Measure how the period of a simple pendulum depends on its length, and check the result against the small-angle prediction T = 2π√(L/g).

  1. Tie the steel bob to a string and measure length L from pivot to the bob's center.
  2. Displace the bob a small angle (< 15°) and release from rest.
  3. Time 10 full swings with a stopwatch; divide by 10 for the period.
  4. Repeat 3 times per length and average.
  5. Shorten the string in ~10 cm steps and repeat for nine lengths.

Reaction-time error on the stopwatch (±0.2–0.3 s per single swing) is the dominant source of uncertainty; timing 10 swings and dividing by 10 reduces its effect roughly tenfold, giving the ±0.02 s per trial shown in the table. Length measurements to the bob's center carry a smaller uncertainty of about ±0.3 cm. The short-length trials show the largest fractional deviation from theory, consistent with reaction-time error mattering more when the period itself is short.

The measured periods track the small-angle prediction closely across the full range of lengths tested. The T ∝ √L relationship holds well within measurement uncertainty, supporting the small-angle model of simple pendulum motion.
